Where to live in France when you work in Geneva

Jérôme Austin· co-founder of La Villa ColivingNovember 2, 2026· Updated November 3, 202613 min

If you work in Geneva, live on the French side close to a Léman Express station or a tram 17 stop: Annemasse, Ville-la-Grand or Ambilly for a job in Eaux-Vives, Champel or the city centre, Ferney-Voltaire and the Pays de Gex for the UN district, the airport or CERN. So the real question isn't "which town?" but "how far am I from a station, a tram stop, or the border crossing I'll use every morning?". Four things decide it: where you work in Geneva, whether you have a car, your budget, and how long you're staying. When people ask us, our answer always starts with another question: where's your office? This guide is about Geneva, not Basel or Lausanne; for the town-by-town list, our comparison of French towns near Geneva takes over.

In short

  • Without a car, pick your station or tram stop first, then the town around it.
  • Eaux-Vives, Champel, Cornavin or the centre: aim for Annemasse, Ville-la-Grand or Ambilly. UN district, airport or CERN: look at Ferney-Voltaire and the Pays de Gex.
  • For 6 to 12 months or a first job, a furnished place with no minimum stay saves you from a year-long commitment to the wrong choice.

First: where you work in Geneva changes everything

Your workplace is half the answer: Geneva stretches from east to west, and the same French town can be one train ride away or a trip right across the city.

Profile 1: you're arriving alone, without a car, and you need it to work from day one

Find a place within walking distance of a Léman Express station or a tram 17 stop: that comes first, before the neighbourhood and before the floor area.

Three areas fit this profile:

  • Annemasse, around the station: the Léman Express gets you to Eaux-Vives in 7 minutes, and everything else is on foot (shops, restaurants, a GP).
  • Ambilly, along tram 17: the town sits right on the border, and the tram runs straight into Geneva.
  • Ville-la-Grand, by the Foron: quiet streets, the river that marks the border close by, and Annemasse station within walking distance.

Arriving alone also means arriving without a network. A flatshare or a coliving solves both at once: a furnished place from the first night, and people to have dinner with. Building a network in Geneva has more ideas for the first few weeks.

Profile 2: you have a car and you work on the right bank, in the UN district or at the airport

Look at Ferney-Voltaire and the Pays de Gex: you're on the right side of Geneva, with no city to cross every morning.

Ferney-Voltaire is popular with people working at the international organisations, the airport and CERN: a historic centre, shops, but a tight market. Further out, the Pays de Gex becomes more residential. If you're joining an international organisation, our guide for UN and IO staff goes through the options.

Before you sign, check three things:

  • the border crossing and the morning queue: do the journey on a weekday, at the time you'll really leave; a time checked on a Sunday tells you nothing about your daily life;
  • parking when you get there: parking in Geneva is expensive, and it's often the biggest item in a car budget (the real cost of cross-border commuting);
  • park-and-ride: leaving the car at the edge of the city and finishing by tram or bus keeps you out of the centre at rush hour.

With a car but a job in Eaux-Vives or Champel, the east side is still simpler: the Léman Express keeps you off the road.

Profile 3: you work from home two days a week and you want space

If you're only in the office three days a week, you can aim for somewhere quieter and roomier: Ville-la-Grand, or Saint-Julien-en-Genevois if your office is around Plainpalais or Onex.

Two things to keep in mind:

  • the 40% threshold: two days of remote work in a five-day week keep your salary taxed at source in Switzerland; above 49.9%, it's your social security cover that can switch to France. The rules, and coworking spaces on the French side, are in our guide to remote work for cross-border workers.
  • a proper desk corner: a room you sleep and work in needs to be big, quiet and bright. Look at the size of the room, not just the size of the home.

Profile 4: your budget is tight, or it's your first job

Compare total cost, not rent: a cheap studio you have to furnish, equip and connect to every utility can cost more in the first year than an all-inclusive room.

For a first job, two traps come up again and again: agency and application fees, and the guarantor in France that agencies often ask for when your file has no French payslips. What application fees really cost and the cross-border rental file help you prepare for both.

A simple benchmark: total housing costs under 30% of your net salary leave you room to breathe. The real cost of a flatshare near Geneva goes through every line, and the ballpark figures are further down this page.

Profile 5: you're coming for 6 to 12 months (assignment, fixed-term contract, internship, probation)

Look for a furnished place with no minimum stay and short notice: a probation period that ends early shouldn't cost you a year's rent.

  • A standard furnished lease runs for a year, and you can leave at any time with one month's notice (French law of 6 July 1989, article 25-8).
  • The "bail mobilité", from 1 to 10 months, is reserved for people in training, on an internship, on a temporary assignment or relocating for work: it can't be renewed, and the landlord can't ask for a deposit (same law, articles 25-12 to 25-18).
  • A furnished flatshare or a coliving: often all inclusive, sometimes with no minimum stay, which is the most flexible option if you don't yet know how long you'll stay.

When it's not the right choice: what we'd steer you away from

We'd rather tell you before you sign: here are four cases where the place you're looking at is likely to cost you every day.

  • A place far from a station or a tram stop, without a car. Beyond a quarter of an hour's walk, it adds to every journey, twice a day, five days a week.
  • A town beyond the Annemasse area for a job in Eaux-Vives. You may save on rent, but you lose on the commute every day.
  • The wrong side of Geneva. Annemasse for a job at CERN, or Ferney-Voltaire for a job in Champel: you cross the city morning and evening.
  • A listing that looks too good. Rent well below the market, a landlord "abroad", payment requested before a viewing: housing scams near Geneva shows the cases we come across.

And if you're coming as a family, or as a couple who want a place of your own, our format isn't the right one: with us, each room is for one person (a couple can take two). Look for a one- or two-bedroom flat instead, and plan on the longer timelines in the table below.

FAQ

How much does a room on the French side cost?

Allow €700 to €1,000 a month for a room in a standard flatshare, depending on the town, with bills often extra (2026). A premium coliving room, all inclusive, starts from CHF 1,370/€1,470 a month. Compare the total cost rather than the rent alone: a cheap place you have to furnish and connect to every utility can end up costing more.

Where should I live if I'm only in Geneva for 6 to 12 months?

Somewhere furnished, with short notice. A standard furnished lease runs for a year, but you can leave at any time with one month's notice; the "bail mobilité", from 1 to 10 months, is for people in training, on an internship, on a temporary assignment or relocating for work. If you don't yet know how long you'll stay, a furnished flatshare or a coliving, often all inclusive, is the most flexible option.

Is Annemasse a good place to live if you work in Geneva?

Yes, especially if you work in Eaux-Vives, Champel or the centre: the station puts you on the Léman Express, and daily life is on foot. For a job at CERN or the airport, the other side of Geneva is a better fit. Living in Annemasse when you work in Geneva covers the details.

How far is Annemasse from the Geneva border?

Very close: Annemasse's neighbours, Ambilly and Ville-la-Grand, share a border with the canton of Geneva. From Annemasse station, the Léman Express reaches Geneva Eaux-Vives in 7 minutes; from a place 10 minutes' walk from the station, allow 20 minutes door to door.

Ferney-Voltaire or Annemasse: which side of Geneva?

The side your office is on. Ferney-Voltaire for the UN district, the airport and CERN; Annemasse for Eaux-Vives, Champel, Cornavin and the centre, by Léman Express.

Do you need to live in Switzerland to get a permit?

No. The G permit is exactly that: as an EU or EFTA national, you work in Geneva and live in a neighbouring country, returning home at least once a week, and you file the application online with the OCPM before your first day at work. The bottom line

On the French side, you choose a commute first, then a town. For a job in Eaux-Vives, Champel or the centre: Annemasse, Ville-la-Grand and Ambilly, near a station or tram 17. For the UN district, the airport or CERN: Ferney-Voltaire and the Pays de Gex. Without a car, the walk to the station matters more than the name of the town. And for a first job or a short stay, a furnished place with no minimum stay gives you time to get to know the area before you commit.
