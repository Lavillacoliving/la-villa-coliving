For a young professional arriving with a contract in Geneva, a room on the French side, in a flatshare or a coliving, costs all-in half to two thirds of a studio in Geneva, for a 20-minute door-to-door commute to the centre, and with more space. The studio remains the right choice if you want to live alone, in town, for two years or more, and your Swiss rental file is already ready. This guide compares the real monthly cost of each housing category, without brands, then decides profile by profile: single and in a hurry, couple, remote worker, tight budget, short stay.

**In short**
- The advertised rent tells you nothing: compare the total cost, bills, internet, furniture, insurance, transport and deposit included.
- All-in, a studio in Geneva lands between 1,500 and 3,000 CHF a month before furniture; a room on the French side between 800 and 1,250 € in a classic flatshare, and from {{PRIX_DES}} plus transport in coliving.
- The right category depends on your profile, not your taste: the length of your stay, the state of your file and the need for a whole home decide before the price does.

## Decision by profile: five situations, five answers

**Single and in a hurry, job starting in thirty days.** Take a room on the French side, in coliving if you want turnkey within one to two weeks, in an aparthotel if you do not yet know where you will really work. A studio in Geneva takes four to eight weeks without a Swiss history, three payslips and a blocked deposit: you will come back to it after three months of pay if you still want it. With us, the median time between application and move-in is thirty days (2026 data), and one week is enough when the room is ready.

**Couple.** You need a whole home: a furnished one-bedroom flat on the French side rents for 900 to 1,150 € a month excluding charges (portals and Observatory, 2026), a one-bedroom in Geneva markedly more. Coliving is not built for two, barring a one-off exception: our rooms are single. A classic flatshare as a couple in a large flat exists, but it is negotiated case by case with the landlord.

**Remote worker.** Three needs: a serious connection, a desk, and people in the evening. Coliving ticks all three, fibre up to 8 Gb/s, a desk in the room, flatmates coming back from Geneva at 7 pm. The studio guarantees silence but isolates you, and internet, desk and going out add to the rent. The classic flatshare depends entirely on the flat and the flatmates you find.

**Budget below 1,200 € a month all-in.** Premium coliving is out of budget, and it is better said plainly: aim for a classic flatshare on the French side, between 800 and 1,250 € all-in, or a large coliving residence if you accept a studio in a block and a car. Our [guide to finding a flatshare near Geneva](/en/blog/trouver-colocation-geneve-frontalier) gives you the groups, the portals and the traps.

**Stay under six months.** Forget the studio in Geneva: twelve-month lease, file, deposit and furniture to resell. Coliving works from three months' commitment, then one month's notice; the aparthotel on the French side covers stays of a few weeks, between 1,300 and 1,600 € a month including charges (listings, September 2026). A classic flatshare is sometimes negotiated as a sublet, with the risks that go with it.

## When the studio in Geneva is the right choice

The studio wins when you meet four conditions: you want to live alone, you are staying two years or more, your Swiss file already exists, three payslips, debt-collection extract and rent guarantee, and you insist on walking to work. In that case, the extra cost buys you total autonomy, a Geneva address and no border in your daily life. It also wins if your employer houses you or finds you a flat, which happens in international organisations and large groups. Many of our residents went the other way round: three months to a year on the French side, the time to build a Swiss file and get to know the neighbourhoods, then a flat in Geneva, or not.

## When it is not the right choice

Coliving on the French side is not for everyone, and saying so avoids pointless visits. If you are coming for a few weeks of tourism or an assignment of under three months, a serviced residence suits you better than a lease. If you arrive as a couple or with a child, you need a whole home, not a room in a shared house. If your total budget is below 1,200 € a month, a classic flatshare will suit you better. If your car is essential every day, the border crossing at rush hour looks nothing like the train times, and a studio in Geneva or a home further from the crossings will make more sense. To compare the French-side towns with each other, read [where to live on the French side when you work in Switzerland](/en/blog/ou-habiter-frontalier-suisse-villes-france-pas-cher).

## Frequently asked questions

**Studio in Geneva or a room on the French side: which costs less, all-in?**

The room on the French side, by a wide margin, once you add everything up. A studio in Geneva rents for 1,200 to 2,500 CHF a month excluding charges according to the listings surveyed in 2026, and you must add charges, electricity, internet, insurance, furniture and a blocked deposit of up to three months' rent. A room in a classic flatshare on the French side comes to 800 to 1,250 € all-in; in coliving, the rent from {{PRIX_DES}} includes everything except transport, i.e. 116.50 € a month with the Léman Pass in 2026.

**Coliving or flatshare: what is the concrete difference?**

A flatshare means sharing a rent: you find the flat, furnish your room, split the bills and the cleaning between flatmates, with a twelve-month lease and often a guarantor. Coliving means sharing a house that is already organised: furnished room, bills, fibre and cleaning of the common areas included, selected residents, a single point of contact, and with us a 12-month lease with a minimum commitment of 3 months, then one month's notice. You pay more per month, and you buy space, comfort and the time you do not spend managing.

**How much does a studio in Geneva really cost per month?**

Count 1,500 to 3,000 CHF a month all-in for a furnished studio, and 1,750 to 3,600 CHF if you have to furnish it, spreading the furniture over one year: rent of 1,200 to 2,500 CHF excluding charges (listings, 2026), charges and electricity of 150 to 280 CHF, internet of 40 to 70 CHF, insurance of 20 to 40 CHF, licence fee of 28 CHF a month (Serafe), tpg pass of 70 CHF (unireso, 2026). The deposit can reach three months' rent on a blocked account (art. 257e of the Swiss Code of Obligations).

**I earn a Swiss salary: can I rent on the French side?**

Yes, with cross-border status and a G permit: for an EU or EFTA citizen, it is requested on the basis of the employment contract from arrival; for a national of another country, the OCPM requires six months of regular domicile in the border zone (page updated 16 February 2026). The details, permit, tax at source and health insurance, are in our guide [moving to Geneva: Swiss side or French side](/en/blog/s-installer-a-geneve-expatrie-cote-suisse-ou-cote-france).

**Do I need a guarantor for a room on the French side?**

In a classic flatshare, very often yes, and a guarantor based in France, which a newcomer does not have. With us, the file is limited to the employment contract or job offer, an ID and the deposit of {{CAUTION_MOIS}} months excluding charges; a guarantor is only discussed case by case, when the contract does not cover the rent, and always before the visit.

**Does the commute count in the budget?**

Yes, and it is easy to put a figure on: from Annemasse, Ville-la-Grand or Ambilly, the Léman Pass monthly Annemasse-Geneva costs 116.50 € in 2026 and central Geneva is 20 minutes door-to-door; in Geneva itself, the unireso Tout Genève pass costs 70 CHF a month (tpg, 2026). The transport gap never overturns the comparison, but it is part of the total cost, like a car if you keep one.
